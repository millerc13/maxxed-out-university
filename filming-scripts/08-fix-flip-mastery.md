# LESSON 1: Choosing the Right Flip

---

## SCRIPT

Not every property is a good flip. Here's how to identify the best candidates.

## Ideal Flip Characteristics

### Location First
- **Best block in a good neighborhood** — Worst house on the best street
- **Strong buyer demand** — Houses selling quickly (low days on market)
- **Good schools** — Families pay premium
- **Low crime** — Check crime maps

### Property Profile
- 3+ bedrooms, 2+ bathrooms (sweet spot for families)
- Standard layout (avoid weird floor plans)
- Cosmetic issues, not structural disasters
- Clear title, no major liens

### Numbers That Work
- Buy at 70% of ARV minus repairs (or better)
- Sufficient margin for unexpected costs
- Realistic ARV based on recent comps

## Properties to AVOID

🚫 **Weird layouts** — Split levels, tiny bedrooms, poor flow
🚫 **Busy roads** — Hard to sell regardless of quality
🚫 **Environmental issues** — Mold, asbestos, lead (expensive to remediate)
🚫 **Foundation problems** — Unpredictable costs
🚫 **Declining neighborhoods** — Values dropping
🚫 **Overimproved for the area** — Can't recoup costs

## Analyzing the Market

### Check Days on Market (DOM)
- Under 30 days: Hot market, price aggressively
- 30-60 days: Normal market, price at comps
- 60+ days: Slow market, be conservative

### Comp Velocity
How many similar homes sold in last 3-6 months? More sales = more confidence in ARV.

### Price Per Square Foot
Know your market's $/sqft for renovated homes. This gives you a quick sanity check on ARV.

## The Golden Rule

**When in doubt, walk away.**

There will always be another deal. One bad flip can wipe out profits from several good ones.

# LESSON 2: Flip Budgeting & Scope of Work

---

## SCRIPT

Accurate budgeting separates profitable flippers from broke ones. Here's how to build a reliable rehab budget.

## Line-Item Budgeting

Break down every cost into categories:

### Exterior
- Roof: $________
- Siding/paint: $________
- Windows: $________
- Doors: $________
- Landscaping: $________
- Driveway/walkways: $________
- Gutters: $________

### Interior - Structure
- Framing/walls: $________
- Insulation: $________
- Drywall: $________
- Flooring: $________
- Interior doors: $________
- Trim/baseboard: $________

### Kitchen
- Cabinets: $________
- Countertops: $________
- Appliances: $________
- Backsplash: $________
- Sink/faucet: $________

### Bathrooms
- Vanity/sink (each): $________
- Toilet (each): $________
- Tub/shower (each): $________
- Tile: $________
- Fixtures: $________

### Mechanicals
- Electrical: $________
- Plumbing: $________
- HVAC: $________
- Water heater: $________

### Other
- Permits: $________
- Dumpster: $________
- Cleaning: $________
- Staging: $________

## The Scope of Work Document

Create a detailed document that describes exactly what work will be done:

- **Room by room** breakdown
- **Materials specified** (e.g., "LVP flooring, gray, $2/sqft")
- **Finishes described** (e.g., "White shaker cabinets, 36" upper")
- **Dimensions** where needed

This document:
1. Gets you accurate contractor bids
2. Prevents scope creep
3. Holds contractors accountable
4. Helps you compare bids apples-to-apples

## Change Orders Kill Profit

A change order is any work added after the contract is signed.

### How to Minimize Change Orders
- Do thorough inspection before budgeting
- Include contingency in budget
- Make decisions BEFORE construction starts
- Stick to the plan

### When Change Orders Happen
- Document everything
- Get pricing in writing before approving
- Understand how it affects timeline

## Contingency: Your Safety Net

**Always add 10-20% contingency** to your budget.

- Light rehab: 10% contingency
- Heavy rehab: 15-20% contingency

If your line items total $40,000, budget $44,000-$48,000.

# LESSON 3: Managing Contractors & Timelines

---

## SCRIPT

Your contractors can make or break your flip. Here's how to find, manage, and pay them properly.

## Finding Good Contractors

### Where to Look
- Referrals from other investors (best source)
- Local REI meetups
- Facebook groups
- Home Depot/Lowe's parking lots (for handymen)
- Thumbtack, Angi (use carefully)

### Vetting Process
1. Get 3+ bids for comparison
2. Check references (call them!)
3. Verify insurance and license
4. Look at past work (photos or in person)
5. Start with a small job before big projects

## Bids vs Estimates

### Estimate
A rough guess that can change.

### Bid/Quote
A firm price for specified work.

**Always get written bids with:**
- Detailed scope of work
- Materials included
- Timeline
- Payment terms

## The Contractor Agreement

Get everything in writing:

- Scope of work (detailed)
- Total price
- Payment schedule
- Timeline with milestones
- Change order process
- How disputes are handled
- Insurance requirements

## Payment Schedule

**Never pay everything upfront.**

### Typical Draw Schedule
- 10-20% at contract signing (materials)
- 30-40% at rough completion (demo, framing, rough plumbing/electrical)
- 30-40% at substantial completion
- 10-20% at final walkthrough (punch list complete)

### The Final Payment Rule
Hold final payment until ALL punch list items are done and you're satisfied.

## Managing the Timeline

### Weekly Check-Ins
- Visit the property weekly (or more)
- Document progress with photos
- Address issues immediately

### Timeline Tracking
Create a simple calendar:
- Week 1-2: Demo, rough work
- Week 3-4: Mechanicals, drywall
- Week 5-6: Finishes, paint
- Week 7-8: Punch list, cleaning

### When Things Go Wrong
- Communicate early and often
- Document everything
- Have backup contractors ready
- Know when to cut losses

# LESSON 4: Selling the Flip for Maximum Profit

---

## SCRIPT

All your profit is made (or lost) at the sale. Here's how to maximize your selling price and minimize time on market.

## Staging: Yes or No?

### Full Staging
Furniture, decor, the whole setup.
- Cost: $2,000-$5,000+
- Best for: Higher-end flips, competitive markets
- ROI: Often pays for itself in faster sale and higher price

### Light Staging
Key pieces only (bed, couch, dining table).
- Cost: $500-$1,500
- Best for: Mid-range flips
- Good compromise between cost and impact

### No Staging
Empty property.
- Cost: $0
- When it works: Hot markets, lower price points
- Downside: Harder for buyers to visualize

**Rule of thumb:** The higher the price point, the more staging matters.

## Professional Photos

This is non-negotiable. Professional photos:
- Make your listing stand out
- Get more showings
- Justify higher price
- Cost: $150-$400

### Photo Tips
- Shoot during daytime (natural light)
- All lights on
- Clean and declutter
- Wide-angle lens
- Exterior shots matter too

## Pricing Strategy

### Option 1: At Market Value
Price at exactly what comps suggest.
- Attracts serious buyers
- Normal negotiation expected

### Option 2: Slightly Below Market
Price 2-3% under comps to generate buzz.
- Multiple offers possible
- Faster sale
- Often ends up at or above list price

### Option 3: Above Market
Price higher and wait for the right buyer.
- Risk: extended days on market
- Only works in hot markets
- Can backfire (price drops look bad)

**Recommendation:** Price at or slightly below market for fastest turn.

## Working with Agents

### Investor-Friendly Agent
Find an agent who:
- Understands investment properties
- Has sold flips before
- Prices based on data, not emotion
- Responds quickly

### Commission
- Typical: 5-6% (split between listing and buyer agent)
- Negotiate: 4-5% possible, especially for repeat business
- FSBO: Possible but harder to reach buyers

## Handling Inspections

Buyers will request repairs. Your options:

1. **Fix it** — Do the work before closing
2. **Credit** — Reduce price or give closing cost credit
3. **Decline** — Say no (risk: buyer walks)

**Strategy:** Fix safety/structural issues. Negotiate or credit for cosmetic items.

## The Goal

- List to contract: Under 14 days
- Contract to close: 30-45 days
- Total turn time: Under 60 days

The faster you sell, the less holding costs eat your profit.
