# LESSON 1: Single-Family Homes (SFH) Explained

---

## SCRIPT

Single-family homes are standalone properties with one living unit. They're the most common entry point for new investors.

## Pros of Single-Family Homes

1. **Easier to Finance** – Conventional loans with as little as 3-5% down for owner-occupants, or 20-25% for investors
2. **Simpler to Manage** – One tenant, one property, one set of problems
3. **Larger Buyer Pool** – When you sell, both investors AND homeowners can buy
4. **Lower Entry Cost** – Generally cheaper than multifamily in the same area
5. **Easier to Understand** – Most people grew up in single-family homes

## Cons of Single-Family Homes

1. **100% Vacancy Risk** – When your tenant leaves, income goes to zero
2. **Limited Scale** – One deal = one unit. Slower to build portfolio
3. **Price Per Unit** – Often higher cost per door than multifamily

## 5-Step SFH Evaluation

1. **Location Check** – What's the neighborhood? Schools? Crime? Employment?
2. **Rent Estimate** – What do similar properties rent for? (Rentometer, Zillow, local PMs)
3. **Expense Calculation** – PITI, maintenance, vacancy, PM fees
4. **Cash Flow Analysis** – Does it cash flow at least $100-200/month?
5. **Exit Strategy** – Can you sell it if needed? Who's the buyer?

# LESSON 2: Multifamily Properties Explained

---

## SCRIPT

Multifamily properties contain two or more units in one building. This includes duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, and larger apartment buildings.

## Types of Multifamily

- **Duplex:** 2 units
- **Triplex:** 3 units
- **Fourplex:** 4 units
- **Small Multifamily:** 5-20 units
- **Large Multifamily:** 20+ units

**Important:** Properties with 1-4 units are considered "residential" for financing. 5+ units are "commercial" and valued differently.

## Pros of Multifamily

1. **Reduced Vacancy Risk** – One vacancy doesn't mean zero income
2. **Economies of Scale** – One roof, one property tax bill, shared expenses
3. **Forced Appreciation** – Value is based on income (NOI), which you can control
4. **Faster Portfolio Growth** – One deal = multiple units

## Cons of Multifamily

1. **Higher Entry Cost** – More expensive upfront
2. **More Complex Management** – Multiple tenants, more maintenance
3. **Commercial Loans (5+ units)** – Different financing requirements
4. **Smaller Buyer Pool** – When selling, buyers are mostly investors

## 4-Step Multifamily Underwriting

1. **Get the Rent Roll** – List of all units, current rents, lease terms
2. **Review the T12** – Trailing 12 months of income and expenses
3. **Calculate NOI** – Income minus operating expenses (before debt)
4. **Apply Cap Rate** – Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate

# LESSON 3: Which Lane Fits You? Self-Assessment

---

## SCRIPT

Not every investor should pursue the same strategy. Your path depends on your capital, time, risk tolerance, and goals.

## Three Investor Profiles

### The Builder
- Wants active income NOW
- Willing to hustle for deals
- Less capital, more time
- **Best strategies:** Wholesaling, Fix & Flip

### The Operator
- Wants passive income over time
- Has capital to deploy
- Less time, wants systems
- **Best strategies:** Buy & Hold Rentals, BRRRR

### The Hybrid
- Wants both active and passive income
- Balances time and money
- Flips to fund rentals
- **Best strategies:** Flip → BRRRR combo

## 5 Questions to Find Your Lane

Answer these honestly:

1. **How much capital do you have to invest?**
   - Under $20K → Wholesaling, partnerships
   - $20K-$100K → BRRRR, small flips
   - $100K+ → Multifamily, multiple deals

2. **How much time can you commit weekly?**
   - 5-10 hours → Buy & hold with PM
   - 10-20 hours → BRRRR, light wholesaling
   - 20+ hours → Active flipping, wholesaling

3. **What's your risk tolerance?**
   - Low → Rentals, long-term holds
   - Medium → BRRRR
   - High → Flips, creative deals

4. **Do you need income now or later?**
   - Now → Wholesaling, flipping
   - Later → Rentals, BRRRR

5. **What's your 5-year vision?**
   - Replace job income → Focus on cash flow
   - Build wealth → Focus on equity and appreciation
   - Both → Hybrid approach
