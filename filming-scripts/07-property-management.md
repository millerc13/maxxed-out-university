# LESSON 1: Screening Tenants Like a Pro

---

## SCRIPT

Bad tenants are usually a screening problem, not a luck problem. Here's how to screen properly.

## The Screening Mindset

**"I'd rather have a vacant unit than a bad tenant."**

Bad tenants cost you:
- Missed rent
- Property damage
- Eviction costs
- Stress and time

## Your Screening Criteria (Write It Down)

Create written criteria and apply it consistently to every applicant:

### Income Requirement
- Minimum 3x monthly rent in gross income
- Verified through pay stubs, tax returns, or bank statements

### Credit Requirements
- Minimum score: 580-650 (depends on your market)
- Look for patterns: late payments, collections, bankruptcies
- Medical debt is less concerning than credit card debt

### Rental History
- No evictions (firm requirement)
- Positive references from previous landlords
- Stable housing history (not moving every 6 months)

### Background Check
- No violent felonies
- Follow local fair housing laws on criminal history
- Drug-related convictions: case by case

### Employment
- Currently employed OR sufficient assets/income source
- Verify with employer if possible

## The Screening Process

1. **Pre-screen** — Phone questions before showing
2. **Show property** — Observe their questions and attitude
3. **Application** — Collect all required documents
4. **Run checks** — Credit, criminal, eviction
5. **Verify** — Call employer and previous landlords
6. **Decision** — Apply criteria consistently

## Landlord Reference Questions

Call previous landlords and ask:
- Did they pay rent on time?
- Did they give proper notice?
- Any lease violations?
- Would you rent to them again?

## Fair Housing Compliance

Apply the same criteria to every applicant. Document your decisions. Never discriminate based on protected classes.

# LESSON 2: Lease Agreements That Protect You

---

## SCRIPT

Your lease is your protection. A good lease prevents problems; a bad lease creates them.

## Essential Lease Clauses

### 1. Names and Occupants
List every adult who will live there. Unauthorized occupants = lease violation.

### 2. Rent Terms
- Amount due
- Due date (usually 1st of month)
- Acceptable payment methods
- Where to pay

### 3. Late Fees
- Grace period (3-5 days is common)
- Late fee amount ($50 or 5% of rent)
- Daily late fee if applicable

### 4. Security Deposit
- Amount collected
- What it covers
- Conditions for return
- State law requirements

### 5. Lease Term
- Start and end date
- Month-to-month conversion terms
- Notice required to vacate (30-60 days)

### 6. Maintenance Responsibilities
- Tenant: light bulbs, filters, lawn care, etc.
- Landlord: major repairs, appliances, structure

### 7. Pets
- Allowed or not
- Pet deposit/fee
- Breed restrictions
- Number limit

### 8. Property Rules
- Noise restrictions
- Parking rules
- Smoking policy
- Guest policy

### 9. Default and Remedies
- What constitutes default
- Cure period
- Eviction process reference

## House Rules Addendum

Attach a separate document with detailed rules:
- Quiet hours
- Parking assignments
- Trash procedures
- Common area rules (for multi-unit)

## State-Specific Requirements

Lease laws vary by state. Always:
- Use a state-compliant lease
- Include required disclosures (lead paint, mold, etc.)
- Follow security deposit rules
- Have an attorney review your lease

# LESSON 3: Maintenance & Systems

---

## SCRIPT

Maintenance is where landlords lose money — or save it. Systems make the difference.

## Reactive vs Preventive Maintenance

### Reactive Maintenance
Fixing things when they break. Necessary but expensive if it's your only approach.

### Preventive Maintenance
Scheduled maintenance to prevent breakdowns:
- HVAC filter changes (quarterly)
- Gutter cleaning (twice/year)
- Water heater flush (annually)
- Roof inspection (annually)
- Pest control (quarterly or as needed)

**Preventive maintenance costs less than emergency repairs.**

## Emergency Protocol

Define what's an emergency:
- No heat in winter
- No water
- Gas leak
- Flooding
- Security issue (broken door/window)

Have a plan:
1. 24/7 contact number for tenants
2. Vendor list for each emergency type
3. Clear authorization for emergency spending limits

## Work Order System

Even with one property, track maintenance requests:

### What to Track
- Date received
- Description of issue
- Priority level
- Vendor assigned
- Date completed
- Cost

### Tools
- Simple spreadsheet
- Property management software (Buildium, AppFolio, RentRedi)
- Even a shared Google Doc

## Building Your Vendor List

For each category, have 2-3 trusted vendors:
- Plumber
- Electrician
- HVAC technician
- Handyman
- Appliance repair
- Locksmith
- Cleaning service

### Finding Good Vendors
- Ask other landlords
- Check reviews
- Start with small jobs
- Pay promptly to keep them loyal

# LESSON 4: Self-Manage vs Hire a Property Manager

---

## SCRIPT

One of the biggest decisions landlords face: manage yourself or hire a professional?

## Self-Management

### Pros
- Save 8-10% of rent
- Total control over decisions
- Direct relationship with tenants
- Learn the business deeply

### Cons
- Time commitment
- Need to learn landlord-tenant law
- Handle difficult situations yourself
- Harder to scale

### Best For
- Local properties
- 1-10 units
- Investors who want to learn
- Those with flexible schedules

## Hiring a Property Manager

### Pros
- Hands-off income
- Professional screening and leasing
- Established systems and vendors
- Easier to scale
- Out-of-state investing possible

### Cons
- 8-10% of rent (plus leasing fees)
- Less control
- Need to manage the manager
- Quality varies widely

### Best For
- Out-of-state properties
- 10+ units
- High-income investors (time > money)
- Those who want passive income

## What Property Managers Do

### Typical Services
- Marketing and showing units
- Tenant screening
- Lease signing
- Rent collection
- Maintenance coordination
- Tenant communication
- Eviction filing (if needed)

### Typical Fees
- Monthly management: 8-10% of collected rent
- Leasing fee: 50-100% of first month's rent
- Maintenance markup: 10-20% on repairs

## Finding a Good Property Manager

### Questions to Ask
1. How many units do you manage?
2. What's your vacancy rate?
3. How do you screen tenants?
4. How do you handle maintenance?
5. What's your eviction process?
6. How do you communicate with owners?
7. Can I see a sample management agreement?
8. Can you provide references?

### Red Flags
- Won't provide references
- Vague about fees
- Poor communication during interview
- No clear systems

## The Hybrid Approach

Some investors:
- Self-manage locally
- Use PMs for out-of-state properties
- Start self-managing, then hire PM as they scale

**There's no wrong answer — choose what fits your goals and lifestyle.**
