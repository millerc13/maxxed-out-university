# LESSON 2: Real Estate as a Wealth Engine (The 4 Pillars)

---

## SCRIPT

Before you chase any deal, you need to know what makes real estate so powerful.

Real estate isn't just "owning a house." It's a **wealth engine** — a machine that pays you in multiple ways at once.

We'll keep it simple. There are four main pillars:
1. Cash Flow
2. Equity
3. Appreciation
4. Tax Advantages

## 1. Cash Flow

Cash flow is the money left after all bills are paid.

**Example:**
- Rent: $1,500
- Mortgage: $900
- Taxes & insurance: $200
- Repairs/maintenance reserve: $100
- Property management: $150
- **Total expenses = $1,350**
- **Cash flow = $1,500 – $1,350 = $150 per month**

One property at $150/month isn't life-changing. But:
- 10 properties → $1,500/month
- 25 properties → $3,750/month
- 40 properties → $6,000/month

That's how people replace jobs.

## 2. Equity

Equity is the difference between what a property is worth and what you owe.

If your property is worth $250,000 and your loan is $180,000, you have **$70,000 in equity**.

What's cool? Every month:
- Your tenant pays rent
- Part of that payment goes toward your loan
- Your equity grows, quietly, in the background

That's forced savings and wealth.

## 3. Appreciation

Appreciation is the property going up in value over time.

You might buy a house for $200,000, and five years later it's worth $260,000. You gained $60,000 just by existing.

Is it guaranteed? No. Markets move. But over long periods, real estate has historically gone up in value.

## 4. Tax Advantages

The fourth pillar is the tax side — and this is where real estate crushes a regular job.

With real estate, you can:
- Depreciate the building (even while it's going up in value)
- Write off interest
- Write off certain repairs and expenses
- Potentially use advanced strategies like bonus depreciation and cost segregation with a good CPA

**Bottom line:** With real estate, you don't just make money — you keep more of it.

## Recap

- **Cash flow** pays your monthly bills
- **Equity and appreciation** build long-term wealth
- **Tax benefits** let you keep more of what you earn

That combination is what makes real estate one of the greatest wealth vehicles available to everyday people.

# LESSON 3: Core Terms Every Investor Must Know

---

## SCRIPT

This lesson is about vocabulary. If you don't speak the language, you'll get lost in conversations with lenders, agents, and contractors.

## Essential Real Estate Terms

### ARV (After Repair Value)
The estimated value of a property **after** renovations are complete. This is what you could sell it for or refinance it at.

**Example:** You buy a property for $150,000, put $30,000 into repairs, and it's now worth $220,000. The ARV is $220,000.

### Equity
The difference between what a property is worth and what you owe on it.

**Example:** Property value = $300,000, Mortgage balance = $200,000, Your equity = $100,000

### Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate)
A way to measure the return on an investment property, calculated as NOI ÷ Property Value.

**Example:** If NOI is $24,000 and the property is worth $300,000, cap rate = 8%

### NOI (Net Operating Income)
The income from a property **before** debt service (mortgage payments). Revenue minus operating expenses.

**Example:** Rent = $36,000/year, Expenses = $12,000/year, NOI = $24,000

### Cash-on-Cash Return
The annual cash flow divided by the total cash you invested. Shows the return on your actual money in the deal.

**Example:** You put $50,000 down, property cash flows $5,000/year. Cash-on-cash = 10%

### Hard Money
Short-term loans from private companies, secured by real estate. Higher interest rates but faster to close. Used for flips and BRRRRs.

### Private Money
Loans from individuals (not institutions). Could be family, friends, or other investors. Terms are negotiable.

### Assignment Contract
A contract that allows you to transfer your rights to purchase a property to another buyer. The backbone of wholesaling.

### Rehab Budget
The estimated cost to renovate a property. Should include materials, labor, and contingency.
