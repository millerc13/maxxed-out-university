# LESSON 1: MLS vs Off-Market Deals

---

## SCRIPT

To find deals, you need to understand where they come from. The two main sources are MLS (on-market) and off-market deals.

## What is the MLS?

MLS stands for **Multiple Listing Service** — the database that real estate agents use to list properties for sale.

### Pros of MLS Deals
- Easy to access (through an agent)
- Professional photos and disclosures
- Clear pricing and terms
- Lender-friendly (traditional financing works)
- Due diligence period built in

### Cons of MLS Deals
- High competition
- Retail pricing (everyone sees the same deals)
- Less negotiation room
- Harder to find "home run" deals

## What are Off-Market Deals?

Off-market deals are properties NOT listed on the MLS. The seller hasn't hired an agent yet — or doesn't plan to.

### Types of Off-Market Situations
- Tired landlords ready to sell
- Pre-foreclosure homeowners
- Inherited properties (probate)
- Divorce situations
- Code violation properties
- Vacant or abandoned properties

### Why Off-Market is Powerful
- **Less competition** — you might be the only offer
- **Motivated sellers** — they need to sell, not just want to
- **Better pricing** — more room to negotiate
- **Creative terms** — seller financing, subject-to, etc.

## The Key Insight

**MLS deals can work** — but you're competing on price and speed.

**Off-market deals** require more effort to find, but the spreads are often better.

The best investors have BOTH channels working for them.

# LESSON 2: How to Build a Lead Machine

---

## SCRIPT

Consistent deal flow is the lifeblood of your investing business. Here's how to build a system that generates leads weekly.

## Lead Sources (Pick 2-3 to Start)

### 1. Investor-Friendly Agents
Build relationships with agents who understand investment deals. Tell them your buy box (price range, areas, property type). They'll send you deals.

### 2. Driving for Dollars
Drive through neighborhoods looking for distressed properties — overgrown lawns, boarded windows, code violations. Write down addresses and skip trace the owners.

### 3. Wholesaler Relationships
Wholesalers find deals and sell the contracts. Get on 5-10 wholesaler lists in your market. They do the marketing; you buy the deals that fit.

### 4. Direct Mail
Send letters or postcards to targeted lists: absentee owners, high equity, tax delinquent, probate. Response rate is low (1-3%) but leads are motivated.

### 5. Cold Calling / Texting
Use skip tracing to get owner phone numbers. Call or text with a simple message: "I buy houses. Are you interested in selling?"

### 6. Auctions
Foreclosure auctions, tax sales, and online auctions (Auction.com, Hubzu). Requires cash or proof of funds, but less competition than MLS.

### 7. Networking
REI meetups, Facebook groups, BiggerPockets forums. Let people know what you're looking for. Deals come through relationships.

## The Weekly Lead Goal

**Set a target:** Analyze 10 deals per week minimum.

Most won't work. That's okay. The goal is consistent practice and pipeline building.

## Track Everything

Use a spreadsheet or CRM to track:
- Lead source
- Property address
- Contact info
- Follow-up dates
- Status (analyzing, made offer, under contract, dead)

# LESSON 3: Quick Rehab Estimation System

---

## SCRIPT

You can't analyze deals without knowing rehab costs. This lesson gives you a system to estimate repairs quickly and accurately.

## The Big 5 Rehab Items

These are the most expensive items. Always check them first:

1. **Roof** — $5,000 - $15,000+ depending on size
2. **HVAC** — $3,000 - $8,000 for replacement
3. **Windows** — $300 - $800 per window
4. **Kitchen** — $5,000 (cosmetic) to $25,000+ (full gut)
5. **Bathrooms** — $3,000 (cosmetic) to $15,000+ (full gut) each

## Cosmetic vs Heavy Rehab

### Light Cosmetic ($10-20/sq ft)
- Paint
- Flooring (LVP, carpet)
- Light fixtures
- Hardware
- Landscaping cleanup

### Medium Rehab ($20-35/sq ft)
- Everything above PLUS
- Kitchen/bath updates
- Some electrical/plumbing updates
- Minor structural

### Heavy Rehab ($35-60+/sq ft)
- Full gut renovation
- New roof, HVAC, windows
- Structural work
- New kitchen and baths
- Electrical/plumbing replacement

## The Contingency Buffer

**Always add 10-20% contingency** to your estimate. Things will go wrong. Surprises will happen.

If your estimate is $50,000, budget $55,000-$60,000.

## When to Bring in Contractors

- Get contractor bids on any job over $30K
- Walk properties with your contractor before making offers
- Use their estimates, not just yours

## How This Fits Into Underwriting

Your rehab estimate plugs directly into your offer price:

**Max Offer = ARV × 70% - Rehab Costs - Wholesale Fee (if applicable)**

Bad rehab estimates = bad offers = lost money or lost deals.
